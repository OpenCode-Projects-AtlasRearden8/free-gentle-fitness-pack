Balance Practice

Steadiness is a quiet superpower. This sheet is about practicing it near something sturdy, in footwear or barefoot, for seconds at a time.

Read this one twice: balance work is education, never exercise prescription. Practice only within arm's reach of a counter, sturdy chair, or wall — fingertips resting lightly on it are allowed forever, not just for beginners. Stop and sit if you feel dizzy, and only practice movement your doctor or physical therapist has cleared.
1 Weight shifts
Feet about hip-width, near the counter. Rock gently side to side, then front to back. Ten slow drifts.
2 Heel raises, holding the counter
Lift heels with fingertips for balance, lower with control. 8 slow ones. Calves love us for these.
3 Toe taps
Feet stay put; rock the weight so heels lift, then toes lift. Alternate 12 times. Footwork without going anywhere.
4 Single-leg stand, fingertip grade
One foot down, the other hovering a hair off the floor. Hold as long as comfortable — seconds, not minutes. Both sides.
5 Slow look-left, look-right
Standing steady, slowly turn your head left, then right. Eyes move first, body follows late. 5 each way.
6 Sit down before you're tired
The pro move. Ending the practice while it still feels easy is the most skilled version of it.
